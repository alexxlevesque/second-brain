## Systems thinking

How simple individual behaviour adds up to whole-system outcomes: feedback loops, tipping points, emergence, reflexive markets.

Start here: play [Parable of the Polygons](https://ncase.me/polygons/) (Vi Hart & Nicky Case, ~10 min). It's an interactive version of Schelling's segregation model, and it makes *Micromotives and Macrobehavior* much easier to read.

| # | Book | Why | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Thinking in Systems: A Primer*, Donella Meadows (2008) | The standard entry point: stocks, flows, feedback loops, why systems surprise people | Easy |
| 2 | *Complexity*, M. Mitchell Waldrop (1992) | Story of the Santa Fe Institute; Brian Arthur on economies and markets as complex systems. Good as an audiobook | Easy |
| 3 | *Micromotives and Macrobehavior*, Thomas Schelling (1978) | Paused, come back after 1–2. Core is chapters 1, 3 and 4 (tipping, critical mass, sorting); skim the rest | Hard |
| 4 | Pick one: *Complexity: A Guided Tour*, Melanie Mitchell (2009) | The math side: chaos, networks, emergence | Medium |
| | or *The End of Theory*, Richard Bookstaber (2017) | Markets as complex systems, from a former risk manager | Medium |
