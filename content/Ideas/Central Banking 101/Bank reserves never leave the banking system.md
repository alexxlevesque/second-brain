Reserves are a special form of money that banks hold at the central bank, used only to settle payments with each other. They cannot be lent out to households or businesses — when a bank "lends reserves," the reserves just move to another bank's account at the Fed. This is why flooding the banking system with reserves (as in QE) does not mechanically force new lending into the real economy.
