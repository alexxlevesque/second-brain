Because commercial banks create money when they lend, the banking system is inherently fragile: banks hold illiquid loans but promise depositors instant cash. In a panic the central bank steps in as the lender of last resort, lending freely against good collateral to stop a liquidity crunch from becoming mass insolvency. This backstop is what keeps the money-market plumbing flowing when private lenders flee.
