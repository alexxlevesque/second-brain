Taleb's idea of scalability distinguishes between professions where effort and reward are linear, like a dentist or taxi driver, whose income is capped by hours worked but also protected from catastrophic loss, and professions where they are not, like a writer, musician, or trader, whose work can be reproduced or leveraged without added effort, allowing for massive, non-linear payoffs. Scalable professions produce highly skewed outcomes, where a small number of people capture nearly all the rewards while most earn little. Taleb argues this same scalability is what exposes these fields to Black Swans, rare and unpredictable events with outsized impact, meaning that success in scalable domains often owes far more to luck than people recognize.
