Imagine parents could choose, from among the many children they might naturally have, which one they will have. Each choice would be private, made for the best of reasons, and the sum of them would reshape the population. Sex is the obvious first case. If many families wanted a boy first, the effect would show up in the sex ratio, in the birth order of boys and girls, and a generation later in the marriage market. The more troubling cases involve traits whose value is relative. Height, and perhaps intelligence, help a child only to the extent that other children have less of them. If everyone selects for a little more, nobody ends up ahead, and everyone has spent effort to stay in place. Once selection becomes common, parents who would never have chosen it may feel they must, so as not to leave their children behind. The questions this raises are about what society should permit or forbid, and they cannot be answered by looking at any one family's choice.
