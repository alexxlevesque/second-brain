A small number of model types turn up again and again, in settings that look nothing alike. The thermostat teaches us about feedback and delay: when the response lags behind the condition it responds to, the system overshoots and swings back and forth. The market for lemons shows that when buyers can't tell good from bad, the good withdraws, the average falls, and the market can unravel, whether the product is a used car or an insurance policy. A seminar that people attend only if enough others attend can wither away, or flourish, depending on where it starts. Expectations can make themselves come true, a shared resource can be ruined by people each using it sensibly, and a public good can go unprovided because everyone is waiting for someone else to pay. Once you recognise the skeleton of one of these models in a new problem, you understand a good deal about that problem before you know any of its details.
