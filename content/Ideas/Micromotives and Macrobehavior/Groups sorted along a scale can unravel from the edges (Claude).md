Race and sex sort people into two boxes; age and income line them up along a scale, and that changes how sorting unfolds. Suppose the members of a club each prefer that the average age not stray too far from their own. If the youngest members find the group a little too old and leave, the average rises, the next-youngest are now the ones out of place, and they leave too. The process can unravel until only the oldest remain, though nobody wanted the club to end that way. Income behaves similarly in neighbourhoods, with the extra twist that people may care less about being close to the average than about not being too far below it. What decides whether a mixed group holds together is not the average tolerance but the tolerance of the people at the edges. A population with the same preferences can end up in one mixed group, in several layered groups, or in a cascade of departures, depending on where it starts and who moves first.
