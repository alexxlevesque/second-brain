Some social outcomes are not explained by anybody's behaviour at all; they are explained by arithmetic. However polite or aggressive the players are, when the music stops in musical chairs, the number left standing is the number of players minus the number of chairs. Every purchase is also a sale, every marriage involves two people, and half of any group will always be below its median, no matter how hard everyone works. In a closed system, what flows out of one category has to flow into another, and the shape of a population at any moment is fixed by who arrives, who leaves, and how quickly people move between groups. Before we explain a pattern by anyone's preferences, we should ask whether it could possibly have come out any other way. Often the identity does the explaining, and behaviour can only decide who ends up where within limits the arithmetic has already set.
