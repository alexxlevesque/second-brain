Glasser's central claim: feelings and behaviors don't simply happen to us — we choose our total behavior (what we do, think, feel, and even our physiology) as our best available attempt to meet our needs. Owning behavior as a choice is what makes change possible: if you chose the misery, you can choose something better. This underlies why [[Identity-based habits vote for who you become]] and demands taking [[Responsibility over blame]].
