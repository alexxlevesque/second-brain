Once a person commits to a better plan, Glasser refuses to accept excuses for not following it — not from harshness, but because accepting excuses reinforces the belief that they can't change. The question is never "why did you fail?" but "when will you do it?" This pairs with keeping the [[Focus on the present, not the past]].
