Work is fulfilling, Gladwell argues, when it has three things: autonomy, complexity, and a clear link between effort and reward. Where those hold, hard work feels worthwhile rather than like drudgery — which is part of why those with the *opportunity* for such work put in the hours that [[The 10,000-hour rule of mastery|mastery]] demands. The condition is usually inherited through circumstance, another instance of how [[Observed success hides its real causes]].
