When you are born can matter as much as who you are. Gladwell notes that an outsized share of tech titans were born around 1955 — old enough to catch the personal-computer revolution as young adults, young enough not to be locked into old careers. The same era-luck appears among 19th-century industrialists. It is accumulative advantage on a generational scale.
