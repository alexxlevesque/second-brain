Gladwell popularizes the claim that reaching world-class expertise in a complex field takes roughly 10,000 hours of practice. The point is less the exact number than the scale: elite performers are not simply born gifted — they have accumulated an enormous, often under-appreciated amount of practice, usually enabled by circumstances that gave them the *opportunity* to put in those hours. (The specific figure is contested in later research, but the emphasis on practice volume endures.)
